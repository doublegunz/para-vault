# Hacktoberfest 2026: From Pull Requests to Open Source AI

In our earlier article, [Hacktoberfest dimulai! Yuk ikutan kontribusi.](https://qadrlabs.com/post/hacktoberfest-dimulai-yuk-ikutan-kontribusi), we explored joining Hacktoberfest through open source contributions. Its registration walkthrough reflected the event at that time. Returning participants now have a different starting point: [Hacktoberfest 2026](https://hacktoberfest.com/) has moved away from counting pull requests and toward learning and building with open-source AI. The official theme is **AI belongs to everyone**.

That changes how we prepare for October. This article explains the new format, how to find an activity that fits your interests, and what has been announced about participation and rewards.

*Information checked on September 28, 2026. Online participation milestones were still awaiting further details on that date.*

## Overview {#overview}

This guide is for developers returning after a previous Hacktoberfest and anyone considering joining for the first time. By the end, you should be able to choose a participation route and identify which details to check before registering.

We will cover the change in focus, the available event formats, and practical preparation. Keep a small learning goal in mind while reading. You might want to understand how an AI model runs, meet people experimenting with similar tools, or turn an idea into a small demonstration.

## What Changed in 2026? {#what-changed-in-2026}

Hacktoberfest 2026 is planned to include more than 300 in-person and online community events throughout October. Major League Hacking (MLH) and DEV now run Hacktoberfest, with DigitalOcean as presenting partner. The focus is practical learning with open-source AI and open-weight models.

| Aspect | Earlier format | 2026 direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Central activity | Contributions tracked through pull requests | Community learning, experimentation, and building |
| Participant focus | Completing the contribution challenge | Joining activities and developing skills |
| Subject matter | Contributions across open source projects | Open-source AI and open-weight models |

For a returning participant, preparation starts with choosing something you want to learn and looking for an event where you can explore it with others.

## Why Move Away from Counting Pull Requests? {#why-move-away-from-counting-pull-requests}

The organizers attribute the change partly to AI making low-effort PRs easier to generate, increasing spam and review pressure on maintainers. Their response is to emphasize collaborative learning and useful experimentation.

Consider a proposed documentation fix. Someone still needs to establish whether the instructions are wrong, reproduce the problem, and check that the replacement works. Generating the proposed edit handles only one part of that process. The work of understanding and verifying it remains.

My takeaway is to set a goal you can explain to another person. What did you try? What worked? Where did it fail? Keeping those answers in a short project note gives you something concrete to discuss with other participants and revisit after the event.

## Hack Days, Meet Ups, and Online Participation {#hack-days-meet-ups-and-online-participation}

A local Fest takes place in person on a single day and comes in two formats: Hack Days and Meet Ups. Each offers a different experience, so check which one an event offers before making plans.

### Hack Days

A Hack Day is a mini-hackathon with project building, workshops or speakers, and prizes. This is a good format to consider if you want time to implement an idea with other developers.

For preparation, reduce your idea to one question you can test in a short session. For example, you could explore whether a model can classify a small set of sample questions. Write down how you would recognize a useful result before starting.

### Meet Ups

A Meet Up is a more flexible community gathering, potentially featuring speakers and discussions. The official format does not include project prizes.

Consider this option if your immediate goal is conversation. Bring questions about a tool you have tried or a concept you want someone to explain. A useful outcome could be a clearer learning plan and a few people to keep in touch with.

### Online Participation

Online activities are also planned. As of September 28, the milestones for the online Swag Envelope program had not been detailed. Check the [official FAQ](https://hacktoberfest.com/questions/) for updates before planning around a reward.

### Understanding the AI Terminology

Open-weight models make learned model parameters available, but access to weights alone does not establish that an entire AI system is open source. Under the [Open Source Initiative's definition](https://opensource.org/ai/open-source-ai-definition), open-source AI involves freedoms to use, study, modify, and share, alongside access to the relevant code, parameters, and training-data information.

When choosing a project, inspect what its release actually includes. Look for its license, model documentation, and instructions for running it. Those details help you understand what you can reproduce and share.

## How to Join Hacktoberfest 2026 {#how-to-join-hacktoberfest-2026}

Start with the [Find a Fest directory](https://hacktoberfest.com/fests/), which supports searching by event name or city and finding nearby events. Open a relevant listing and follow its registration link.

Before registering, check the date, location, agenda, and any preparation instructions. If you are browsing from Indonesia, also check the displayed time zone for online activities. Save the event details somewhere you can find them again.

Local Fests use OrganizerHQ for registration and check-in. Hack Days also use OrganizerHQ Challenges for project submissions and judging. Follow the instructions for your selected event when completing those actions.

For a building session, my suggested preparation checklist is short:

- Bring a laptop and charger if the event asks participants to build.
- Read any setup instructions supplied by the organizer.
- Prepare a small idea and sample inputs you can comfortably share.
- Leave time to explain your result and record what you learned.

If you want to organize an event, start with [Host a Fest](https://hacktoberfest.com/host/). Applications are reviewed on a rolling basis. Hack Days can receive support for eligible event expenses; Meet Ups do not receive MLH funding. Read the handbook available on that page before committing to expenses or announcing official status.

## What About T-Shirts and Swag? {#what-about-t-shirts-and-swag}

T-shirts are distributed at in-person events according to availability and local organizers' discretion. A shirt is not guaranteed for every participant, and online participants are not eligible for one.

Participants unable to attend in person may earn a Swag Envelope through online milestones. It contains stickers and other small items; the milestone details were still pending at verification. Shipping is scheduled to begin after the event, with most deliveries expected within 30 to 60 days.

Choose an activity you would still enjoy if a particular item were unavailable. That makes it easier to decide whether an event is worth your time and travel.

## You Can Still Contribute to Open Source {#you-can-still-contribute-to-open-source}

You can continue helping projects you use throughout the year. Start with something you have encountered yourself: an unclear setup instruction, a missing translation, or a bug you can reproduce. Working from that experience gives you a concrete starting point for a conversation with the project's maintainers.

Choose a problem you understand, read the project's contribution guidelines, and explain your proposed change clearly. Be ready to respond to review. A small improvement that someone can verify is a worthwhile outcome, whether you discovered the project during October or months earlier.

## Conclusion {#conclusion}

For this October, pick a learning goal, find a suitable activity, and give yourself something concrete to take away from it.

- **Check the current format.** Use the 2026 event pages when planning participation.
- **Prepare one useful question.** A focused idea gives your conversations and experiments direction.
- **Verify reward details.** Check availability and the latest online milestone information.
- **Keep contributing.** Apply what you learn to projects and communities after the event.
